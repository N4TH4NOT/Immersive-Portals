# Seamless portal follow-up

[English](seamless-26.2.md) · [Français](seamless-26.2.fr.md)

The alpha.6 work addresses defects reported after earlier runtime checks: pitched close views flickering, dark rooms appearing lit, detached first-person cameras, residual player images, terrain leaks and unavailable interaction through portals. This page records implemented acceptance coverage. Consult [PR #1](https://github.com/N4TH4NOT/Immersive-Portals/pull/1) and the corresponding [GitHub Actions run](https://github.com/N4TH4NOT/Immersive-Portals/actions) for the exact revision's passing, failing or unexecuted checks. Configured scenarios and earlier green runs do not establish acceptance for a different revision or a perfect visual transition.

## Rendering and camera

Each offscreen scene owns a lightmap extractor. Only the main game renderer receives the normal vanilla tick, so remote extractors need an initial update and subsequent client-tick updates. The scene retains the destination's native light, sky and ambient values while sharing the viewer's block-light flicker and boss-overlay darkening. This is separate from propagating Sodium terrain invalidations.

Remote client worlds also run `ClientLevel.update()`. In 26.2, merely polling incoming light packets stages their section data without publishing all pending light-engine updates to rendering. The dark-room fixture exposed a server/client mismatch of block light `8` versus `0`; ticking the lightmap alone cannot repair missing world light data.

Authoritative server light corrections are also forwarded to every loaded remote viewer. A chunk snapshot can precede server relighting, especially when remote worlds are recreated after returning through a portal; that snapshot must not leave the destination permanently dark. The ordinary boundary-only filter for the player's primary world is preserved.

Player bodies use a clipping pass independent of the portal view's own plane. Body color and depth are rendered into an isolated target, then composed with both body and view half-spaces. A geometric camera-inside-body guard prevents rendering the local head around a first-person eye; legitimate distant views of the player through another portal remain possible. Third-person camera collision follows the native eight corner rays through up to five openings, including corners already across the plane, and respects real destination obstacles.

The optical fixture sweeps eye distances `0.9`, `0.3`, `0.04` and `0.001` blocks, camera pitches `30`, `45`, `60` and `89` degrees, and two yaw angles. Consecutive images check the aperture, source/destination occlusion and hidden terrain sentinels. A sealed dark/lit/dark-again destination is compared with direct native views using actual lamp updates and smooth lighting. Separate body scenes exercise remote self visibility, straddling, crouching and front/back third-person views. HUD/tutorial pixels are excluded from body-color measurements.

## Interaction and pickup

Native block and entity targeting uses one shared ray model on the client and server, bounded to five portal crossings and the player's normal interaction range. Portal scaling contributes to distance. Blocks and entities occlude the ray, including at the exit; the server recomputes the claimed path from loaded geometry. The block selection outline belongs only to the matching remote view. Mining retains the target world when local and remote targeting change; its whole server tick, including delayed completion, uses that recorded world for progress, breaking and callbacks. Container validity rechecks the accessible path.

The native-input fixture now has 20 phases using real use/attack keys. It checks containers, buttons, placing, breaking, entity use/attack, source and destination obstructions, aperture bounds, reach and mining across a crossing. Separate projected-body use/attack cases target a visible half whose entity remains anchored in the other world. Bucket cases collect the real destination fluid within normal survival reach and reject a farther fluid source that the former fixed five-block range would allow.

Ordinary and hanging signs open their native editors in the destination world. Their cases type through real keyboard callbacks and activate the native Done button; the server requires the destination text to change while a same-position source sign stays untouched. The edit session retains the validated world and portal path through vanilla's asynchronous text filter. These cases do not establish arbitrary administrative editors or every block-entity menu.

The remaining native phases cover persistent entity actions and attack side effects. A remote trade must stay open for at least 25 client ticks, then close when a real source-side obstruction blocks access. Mounting a destination boat performs a synchronized transfer so rider and vehicle belong to the same world; it cannot leave a cross-world passenger link. Scoped attack geometry restores the player's physical world for reflected damage, death and drops. The lethal Thorns witness requires the real inventory to drop in the physical source world and nowhere in the destination. These are implemented acceptance cases whose outcomes require matching-commit runtime evidence. They do not establish every vehicle, merchant or damage source.

Automatic item pickup extends the vanilla pickup volume through one accessible opening. It uses the actual aperture, the item's visible portion and vanilla `playerTouch` rules for delay, ownership, inventory space, statistics and removal. One authoritative collection cannot award the same stack twice. Pickup animation includes a server-provided destination anchor for observers who do not have the collector in their loaded world. The quality fixture covers approach, delay, ownership, a full inventory, aperture/range rejection, a two-player race and cross-dimension collection.

## Sound and destination environment

Audio selects the shortest eligible direct or single-portal route within a bounded search radius of at most 128 blocks. The virtual source points toward the opening and encodes the full folded acoustic distance, including portal scale, while native gain, pitch and attenuation remain in charge. This does not implement arbitrary multi-portal acoustic propagation, sound occlusion or diffraction.

Offscreen fog keeps its own atmospheric rain interpolation per renderer and client world. Remote particle engines tick once per remote-world tick, independently of how many portal views are extracted. The environment fixture observes actual redirected sound packets and native sound instances for three routes, counts a remote particle's ticks, and compares a wet Overworld with a dry Nether. It does not establish every biome, fluid or weather transition.

## Poses and held item use

Seven native-input traversal cases cover sprinting, crouching, flying, falling, swimming, charging/releasing a bow and eating. Both client and server independently observe the active pose or use on each side, while camera observations reject detachment or added crossing motion. The bow and food cases cross dimensions with use held throughout. Bow release must consume one arrow, damage the bow and hit a real destination target; eating must consume one apple and restore hunger. The seven cases are included in the expanded suite; compilation alone does not establish their runtime acceptance for the exact commit under review.

The Fabric crossing packet snapshots source feet and pose. The server reconstructs eye offsets from its own dimensions, scale and gravity, including destination previews, so a later posture update cannot shift the physical crossing anchor. Client and Fabric server must use the same mod build; older crossing payloads are incompatible. The version-2 Minestom bridge framing is unchanged. See [network compatibility](network-audit-26.2.md).

## Two players, persistence and limits

The quality scenario connects two distinct real clients to a dedicated server. They approach, stop, cross in opposite directions and return using normal input. Its seven entity witnesses are a mob, a dropped item, two arrows, a cross-dimension mob, a cross-dimension snowball and a cross-dimension small fireball. They check natural trajectories and destination impacts, entity UUID/NBT preservation, and the fireball's native acceleration/drag without integrating motion twice. The world is saved with both players straddling openings, then the server and both clients restart; saved portal identities, positions and inventories are checked before another natural crossing.

Native `Entity.move` now prepares portal contacts from the swept body bounds before resolving collisions, using the same finite-opening rules as authoritative player movement packets. This addresses newly spawned mobs or items losing their first movement to a backing block before end-of-tick contact discovery. Dropped items also prepare contacts before vanilla's earlier collision/ejection query, so a clipped body behind the opening does not trigger random push-out or temporary no-physics movement. Backing blocks outside the opening retain their collisions. The seven quality witnesses exercise these paths; acceptance requires runtime results for the exact commit.

Client frame-time and server tick-time limits are measured on Mesa software runners. They are regression budgets, not a performance promise for a player's GPU. Destination readiness and preload behavior must not be inferred solely from the absence of a crash.

## Fluid contact and projectile segments

Native `EntityFluidInteraction` sampling splits the body into its physical source parts and its projected destination part. It merges native fluid heights and eye-submersion flags without changing the entity's world or position. Native force composition combines eligible currents before cutoff, averaging, normalization and minimum-force rules, then transforms the destination contribution and applies the final force once. Hidden source water or lava behind the opening must not push, drown or damage the player, while visible physical water must retain ordinary air depletion. Source body portions beside the finite opening retain their real fluid contact. Remote sampling uses the collision system's axis-aligned bounding-box clipping approximation and the same limit that rejects projected boxes larger than 100 blocks on any axis.

Fluid sampling retains physical source ownership until the actual transfer. Native ticks can advance both server eye snapshots beyond the plane before the client's crossing request transfers the entity; retaining the last validated front-face contact prevents switching to the opposite face and losing destination water contact. This contact is retained only in the same physical world while the body straddles the valid finite opening, and is cleared on teleportation, portal removal, leaving the opening or disabling portal collision. Its acceptance requires matching-commit runtime evidence in PR #1 and Actions.

Nine live-fluid cases observe `hidden_water`, `hidden_lava`, `visible_water`, `destination_water`, `scaled_water` with portal scale 2, `rotated_water` with a 90-degree Y rotation, `destination_lava`, `narrow_edge_water` and `nonplayer_lava`. Projected lava preserves the destination world's native current strength. The finite-edge case keeps real source water contact beside a narrow aperture; actual dropped items check non-player lava normalization and shallow-current cutoff. Both client and server inspect actual native fluid state. Three additional independent force contracts compare composition against an unscoped native tracker: combined non-player normalization, a single player minimum-current rule and destination minimum force before scale conversion. Their outcomes require matching-commit runtime evidence; these cases do not establish all rotations, fluid types or custom aperture shapes.

Native projectile handling splits a tick's collision segment at portal boundaries, resolves the source and remaining destination portions with native collision behavior, and preserves the transformed motion without a second integration. It handles arrow, spectral-arrow and trident families, including their no-physics path; throwable eggs, snowballs, pearls, experience bottles and potions; and hurting projectiles such as fireballs, wither skulls and wind charges. Paths are bounded to eight crossings per tick. The quality witnesses directly exercise arrows, a snowball and a small fireball rather than every family variant.

Fishing hooks, firework rockets, llama spit and shulker bullets retain their inherited server teleportation path. They are not removed or declared wholly unsupported, but their line, attachment, homing and other special behavior is not established by the new native-segment checks.

Implemented expanded coverage includes the seven pose/use cases, nine fluid cases, three independent force contracts and 20 native interaction phases.
